Private Shield

Hides the websites you visit from the wifi you're on, and blocks trackers, ads and scam sites.

How this works

Before your Mac opens any website, it first asks "where does mybank.nl live?". Normally that question goes out in plain text, so whoever runs the wifi (a café, a hotel, your internet provider) can read a list of every site you visit, and a scammer on the same wifi can send you a fake answer.

The shield sends that question encrypted to Mullvad, a Swedish privacy company that keeps no logs. Mullvad also refuses to answer for known trackers, ad networks and scam sites, so they simply never load.

It is a setting inside macOS itself. Nothing extra runs on your Mac and nothing can crash. The file is made fresh on this Mac, just for you.

Honest limits: it is not a VPN. Websites still see your internet address, and your provider can still see which servers you connect to, just not the names you look up.

Testing your shield…

Turn it on (about 2 minutes)

  1. Click Create my shield. System Settings opens on the Device Management page (on older Macs it's called Profiles).
  2. Double-click DataSecure Private Shield.
  3. Click Install and type your Mac password. macOS will say "Unverified". That's normal: it means the file was made on your own Mac instead of bought from a company.
  4. Come back here and click Test my shield. It should turn green.

Turn it off

Open System Settings → General → Device Management, click DataSecure Private Shield, then the minus (−) button.

Café or hotel wifi login page won't appear? Turn the shield off, log in to the wifi, then create the shield again.

Leaked passwords

Companies get hacked every week. When yours does, change that password straight away, or delete the account.

1. Is my password leaked?

How this works

Your Mac turns the password into a scrambled fingerprint and sends only the first 5 characters of it to the leak database (Have I Been Pwned). The database sends back every leaked fingerprint starting with those 5 characters, hundreds of them, and your Mac compares them itself. Your password never leaves this Mac, not even to DataSecure.

2. Get an email the moment your email address leaks

How this works

Two free, trusted services watch every new hack and email you when your address shows up. Sign up for both, once. When an email from them arrives, change the password of that website, and anywhere you used the same password.

3. Watch the websites you use

How this works

Tick the websites you have an account with. Once a day DataSecure downloads the public list of new hacks. If one of your websites is on it, a notification pops up on your Mac telling you to change that password.

No test notification? Open System Settings → Notifications → Script Editor and turn on Allow notifications. (macOS shows DataSecure alerts under that name.)

4. Let your Mac warn you about leaked passwords

  1. Click Open Passwords.
  2. In the menu bar choose Passwords → Settings.
  3. Tick Detect Compromised Passwords. Leaked ones now show under Security in the sidebar. Change each one.

Where my data goes

Right now, live: which app on this Mac talks to which company, and in which country.

How this works

Every app talks to servers on the internet. DataSecure asks macOS which connections are open and looks up who owns each server in a table stored on your Mac, so nothing about your connections is sent anywhere.

Yellow rows are companies DataSecure doesn't recognise. Usually that's fine, for example a website you have open. Don't recognise the app? Search its name online, or ask someone you trust.

Good to know: big hosting firms (Amazon, Cloudflare, Akamai, Google) run servers for thousands of other companies, so "Amazon" often means "a website hosted at Amazon". The country is where the company is registered, not always where the server stands. Apps from macOS itself are only partly shown.

Stay invisible

Get out of advertising profiles, people-search results and data-broker databases.

1. Switch off ad tracking (about 15 minutes, once)

How this works

Google, Meta, LinkedIn, Microsoft and Apple build a profile of you to sell targeted ads. Each has a settings page where you can turn that off. Click each link, sign in, switch everything off, then tick it here so you remember.

2. Ask data brokers to delete you

How this works

Data brokers collect and sell information about people: what you buy, where you live, what you might like. In the EU the law (GDPR, article 17) says they must delete your data when you ask, within one month.

Type your name and email below. DataSecure writes the legal letter for you. For each company, click Email letter (your mail app opens with everything filled in, you press Send), or Copy letter and paste it into their form. DataSecure never sends anything and doesn't store your name.

Some companies reply asking you to prove who you are. If they ask for a copy of your ID, use the free KopieID app from the Dutch government to black out your photo and BSN first.

Clear browser data

Wipe your history, cookies and cache. Do it after banking on a shared computer, or once a month.

How this works

Websites leave three things on your Mac: history (a list of every page you visited), cookies (small files that keep you logged in, and let advertisers recognise you across sites) and cache (copies of pages, including pages from your bank). Clearing them makes you a stranger to trackers again.

You will be logged out of websites afterwards, so make sure you know your passwords first. Your saved passwords are not deleted.

Safari
  1. Click Open Safari.
  2. In the menu bar choose History → Clear History…
  3. Choose all history and click Clear History. This also removes cookies and cache.
Google Chrome
  1. Click Open Chrome.
  2. Press ⌘ Command + Shift + Delete together.
  3. Set Time range to All time, tick all three boxes, click Delete data.
Firefox
  1. Click Open Firefox.
  2. Press ⌘ Command + Shift + Delete together.
  3. Choose Everything, tick all boxes, click Clear.

How DataSecure works

In plain words: what it does, what it never does, and how to remove it.

What it is

DataSecure is a small program that runs only on this Mac. This page is not on the internet: the address 127.0.0.1:7777 means "this computer". Bookmark it (⌘ Command + D) to come back any time.

It checks your Mac's safety settings and shows you how to fix them. It never changes a setting behind your back: every fix is you clicking a switch in System Settings, and it never asks for your Mac password.

What leaves your Mac (and nothing else)

  • The first 5 characters of a password's scrambled fingerprint, when you check a password. Never the password.
  • A daily download of the public list of hacked companies (from haveibeenpwned.com). Nothing about you is sent.
  • A monthly download of the public table of who owns which server (from iptoasn.com). Nothing about you is sent.
  • If you turn on the shield: your website lookups, encrypted, to Mullvad, which keeps no logs.

Words you'll see

  • FileVault: locks everything on your disk with your password. A thief who takes the Mac can't read your files or banking documents.
  • Firewall: a door that stops other computers on the same wifi from connecting to yours.
  • Data breach / leak: a company got hacked and its customers' emails and passwords were stolen.
  • Data broker: a company that collects information about people and sells it.

The 3 habits that matter most

  1. A different password for every website. The Passwords app on your Mac remembers them for you.
  2. Turn on two-step login (a code on your phone) for email and banking.
  3. Your bank will never ask for your password or codes by phone, text or email. Hang up and call the number on your bank card.

Remove DataSecure

Open the Terminal app, paste this line and press Enter:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Starfish124/datasecure/v0.1.0/install.sh | bash -s -- --uninstall

Then remove the shield as shown on the Private Shield page.
